# MT7601U

## Captured Wireless Card

- MediaTek MT7601U, 1x1 2.4 GHz 802.11n (b/g/n), 1 TX chain, 1 RX chain.
- `148f:7601` — two generic no-name dongles, referred to here as `antenna` and `superwang`.
- USB 2.0 high speed, bulk endpoints, max packet 512 on all of them.
- EEPROM version 1 on both units.
- Captures: `driver_captures/captures_mt7601u_{superwang,antenna}/` — `capture-1-coldboot`,
  `capture-2-coldboot-longwait`, `capture-3-monitor-chan` (kernel driver), `capture-5-port-init`
  (this port's bring-up), `capture-6-kernel-tx` (kernel injection). Gitignored; re-capture from the
  kernel driver or with the tools in `scripts/chips/mt7601u/`.

## Linux Driver Source

- Upstream: `torvalds/linux`, `drivers/net/wireless/mediatek/mt7601u/`, mainline.
- Tag `v7.2`, commit `237a1c39e8dfd3e1c6f1f023eea37a48ec04cc63`, fetched 2026-10-05.
- Vendored gitignored at `driver_sources/mt7601u-source-v7.2/`: 23 source files + Kconfig +
  Makefile, with a sha256 manifest in its `PROVENANCE.md`.
- Self-contained relative to `mt76/`: only `mt76` helper headers are shared, no `mt76` modules link.
- **Every `file:line` below resolves against v7.2.** `driver_sources/mt7601u-source-v6.19/` is also
  vendored and differs in `mcu.c`, `usb.c` and `usb.h` only; line numbers past `mcu.c:405` and
  `usb.c:277` shift between the two, so a citation checked against the wrong tree lands off by up
  to 13 lines.

## Python Port Details

- Claims 17 VID:PIDs, one per entry of `mt7601u_device_table` (`usb.c:14`). Discovery walks
  `chips/*/__init__.py` and matches on VID:PID alone — the table carries no OUI narrowing.
- **Status on hardware:** connect, EEPROM, firmware download, register init, BBP/RF calibration,
  channel tune 1-14, receive, monitor injection and Active Monitor all work on both units.
  Campaigns confirmed on the air (2026-10-06): deauth, passive handshake capture, PMKID harvest
  and passive PMKID, WPS (10 consecutive PIN attempts at 3-8 s each), and WEP -- ~500 sustained
  IVs/s on ARP replay, 17k IVs cracked in 1m40s, which is ~2.6x the fastest other card in the
  fleet. `wep_lab.py` reports FakeAuth associating on attempt 1 with AP ACKs clean 1:1.
- The autoresponder is measured, not inferred: `scripts/ack/rx_autoack.py --test-card 7601` ACKs a
  spoofed MAC 100/100 with active monitor on and 0/100 with it off, 0/100 to a MAC nobody owns, and
  101/100 on the silicon MAC.
- Receive measured against the kernel driver per unit, ch1-11, 15 s dwell, all four runs cold and
  inside one 19-minute window: beacon rate off the reference AP 7.8/s vs 6.7 and 7.9/s vs 6.0,
  RSSI median -1.2 dB over 68 common APs and -1.0 dB over 58, channel tune 11/11 with 0 silent,
  breadth 86 vs 85 and 69 vs 64. The beacon-rate margin is not a port advantage: `ref2g` faded
  monotonically through the session and wifit3 ran first in each pair, so read that line as parity.
- The verify gates (`scripts/chips/mt7601u/`) replay all six captures with no divergence, covering
  bring-up plus 59 channel tunes, 4-5 `mac_work` sweeps and 4 calibration passes; `verify_tx`
  checks capture-6's descriptors field-for-field; `verify_tune` replays 55 recorded tunes.
- Shares the `struct mt76_txwi` descriptor shape with `mt76x0u`, `mt76x2u` and `mt7925au`. No
  shared code with any other chip driver.

**Non-obvious in the port**

- **RX URBs must be submitted between `mcu_cmd_init` and `write_mac_initvals`.** That is where
  `dma.c:517 mt7601u_dma_init` sits. Armed earlier the chip never streams a byte; armed at the end
  of `connect()` it does not either.
- **`__mt7601u_vendor_single_wr` splits every 32-bit register write into two 16-bit control
  requests** (`usb.c:157`): low half at `offset`, high half at `offset+2`. In usbmon text the value
  half is the *first* hex group and the register the *second*; reassemble the halves before diffing
  port writes against a capture.
- A readback of a register whose high half lives at `offset+2` returns only the low half, and the
  chip does not answer reads at `offset+2` at all — it times out. `u2me=0x00` from a DW1 read is a
  measurement artifact, not a failed write.
- `MT_TXD_INFO_LEN` is `round_up(txwi + frame, 4)` — it excludes the DMA info word and the zero
  trailer. A capture reads LEN 48 on a 56-byte transfer.
- **The injection endpoint is `out_eps[4]` = 0x07, derived rather than named.** `tx.c` computes
  `q2hwq(0) = 3` then `q2ep(3) = 4`; `usb.h` labels index 2 `MT_EP_OUT_AC_BE`, which is a trap,
  since index 2 carries `qid=1`. Endpoint indices follow descriptor order (`0x08, 0x04, 0x05, 0x06,
  0x07, 0x09`), not ascending. `MT_TXD_PKT_INFO_QSEL` is 2 bits (`GENMASK(26,25)`) and takes
  `ep2dmaq`'s value, not the endpoint index.
- **`MT_TXWI_ACK_CTL_NSEQ` set is what asks the MAC to stamp the sequence number** (`tx.c:160`,
  for `IEEE80211_TX_CTL_ASSIGN_SEQ`). Clear is the kernel's monitor form, which means the MPDU's
  own `seq_ctrl` is transmitted and the caller must supply it — `driver.py:_stamp_tx_seq` does.
  `ack_ctl=0x02` in a capture is NSEQ, not a link-layer ACK request.
- **`MT_TXWI_ACK_CTL_REQ` is set on every injected frame, group addr1 included.** `mt76x0u`:999
  and `mt76x2u`:684 do the same on this identical txwi: the MAC's ACK-based retry is the only
  retransmission injection gets, and active monitor is what lets the chip match an ACK to a spoofed
  Addr2. `ack=False` is replay-only, for byte-matching the aireplay capture -- that capture's
  `ack_ctl=0x00` is aireplay-ng's own radiotap NOACK, not something the kernel imposes, and it
  covers unicast deauth, RTS and auth frames alike. Coupling the bit to the RX ACK tally instead
  starved every `send_no_wait` frame, which is all three WEP components, of any hardware retry.
- `MT_RX_FILTR_CFG` **drops on set**: `main.c:107` sets a bit only when mac80211 did *not* ask for
  that class. `init.c:238` programs the managed default `0x00017f97`; a monitor clears `PROMISC`,
  the `main.c:119` control group and `PSPOLL`, keeping `CRC_ERR`, `PHY_ERR`, `VER_ERR`, `DUP` and
  `RTS`. `RX_FILTER_MONITOR` computes to `0x00001093`, the value the kernel writes in the captures.
- `MT_RXINFO_CRCERR` (`mac.h:54`) is bit 8 of `rxinfo` and is tested in `rx.py:decode_segment`,
  alongside `CRC_ERR` in the MAC filter. Both gates are needed: with them clear, a beacon with a
  flipped byte in the BSSID or the RSN element registers as a second AP with the same SSID.
- `MT_USB_DMA_CFG_RX_BULK_AGG_EN` is set only when `transport.in_max_packet == 512`. That value is
  filled by `assign_pipes()` during `connect()`, long after the driver object exists — read it live,
  never snapshot it at construction.
- `MAX_ACK_DELAY` is overridden to 0.25 s. The ACK round-trip on this chip measures 2.1 ms min,
  57.3 ms median, 119.8 ms max, so the base class's 20 ms window reported a miss on 14 of 15
  genuinely-ACKed frames. Clearing `RX_BULK_AGG_EN` moves the median 60.1 -> 61.2 ms, so the
  latency is this chip's bulk-RX scheduling and not a register knob.
- **`MT_WCID_ATTR_BSS_IDX` is `GENMASK(6, 4)` and `mac.c:359` uses `FIELD_PREP`.** Masking the
  index onto the mask instead of shifting it into the field writes 0 for every `vif_idx`, silently
  attributing every station to BSS 0.
- `init.py:poll` / `poll_msec` reproduce the C's read counts (`core.c:28` / `core.c:50`):
  `timeout // 10 + 1` reads. `poll` adds no delay between reads deliberately — `core.c:42` is
  `udelay(10)`, one vendor-request read already exceeds that, and `time.sleep` cannot deliver 10 us
  on Windows.
- `MT_RX_ORDER` 3 → RX buffers are `PAGE_SIZE << 3` = 32768 bytes.
- `MT_TX_STAT_FIFO` is a free-running counter: read 8 times with nothing in flight it returns
  `0xff23, 0xff22, 0xff22, ...`, decrementing per read. A VALID word appears on a counter wrap, not
  per frame — 74 injected frames yield ~10 statuses. **Not a transmission indicator.**
- TSI/TSSI reads, `rf_rr` and the `_KICK` poll all mutate device state. Reads are not commutative;
  keep their order as the captures have it.
- Channel 14 tunes and receives. `iw` refuses it because the nl80211 regdomain for 2.4 GHz excludes
  2484 MHz; that is a regdomain restriction, not a silicon one, and this port bypasses nl80211.
- `init.c:273-275` reads three registers `regs.h` does not name; they are
  `constants.py:MT_PCNT_0438/0A30/0A34`.
- **`is_kernel_driver_active` is Linux-only and Windows libusb raises `NotImplementedError` from
  it.** `transport.py:claim` and `driver.py:_refuse_kernel_bound_chip` both guard it, per the
  pattern `mt76x0u`, `mt7925au` and `rtl8821au` use. Unguarded, `wlan/interface.py:94` catches the
  `NotImplementedError`, classifies it as a permissions fault, and `device/manager.py:433` then
  offers a WinUSB install — so the card can never connect on Windows and the symptom is a
  "WinUSB needs to be installed" modal on a card already correctly bound to WinUSB. If that modal
  reappears on a bound card, suspect a connect-time exception being reclassified, not the installer.

## Known Problems

- **Key table programming is not ported.** No `MT_KEY_TABLE` writes, so hardware decryption is
  unavailable. This does not block PMKID or WPS: EAPOL M1 and the EAP-WSC exchange are both
  unencrypted, so neither needs the key table, and both are confirmed working on hardware
  (2026-10-06). An earlier version of this entry claimed PMKID was unreachable without a full
  client state machine and that WPS needed AP mode; the gauntlet disproved both.
- **AP mode is not ported.** `mt7601u_add_interface` upstream does MAC-address and group-WCID work
  only, so the gap is the key table above rather than a missing function. EvilTwin does not
  need it -- the campaign software-beacons and runs on this card -- see the Known Problem on it
  below. `eviltwin_modal.py` refuses up front, rather than crashing at compose, only when no
  attached card can host a twin at all; this one can, so it never refuses here.
- **WCID allocation is ported but unproven on air.** `WcidAllocator` ports `main.c:187-204` and
  `wcid_setup` ports `mac.c:355`, with WCID 0 reserved for the broadcast STA (`init.c:583`), the
  monitor WCID 0xff never returned, and allocation stopping at `main.c:203`'s limit of 120. Nothing
  calls `wcid_setup` yet, so this is tested register sequencing only.
- **Superwang hears 13 fewer APs than its own kernel baseline**, all at the noise floor: the 17
  it misses have a median RSSI of -83.0 dBm against -77.6 for shared APs, 9 of them were heard 2 or
  fewer times in 15 s, and the kernel got 130 beacons from all 17 combined. RSSI tracks within
  0.7 dB on common APs, so this is marginal sensitivity, not a decode gap. The antenna unit runs
  the other way, hearing 17 the kernel missed, at least 12 of them more than once.
- **A second process holding the card surfaces as a WinUSB install prompt.** `connect()`
  failing because the device is already claimed is reclassified as a permissions fault by
  `wlan/interface.py`, and `device/manager.py` then offers to install WinUSB on a card that is
  already bound correctly. Same reclassification path as the `is_kernel_driver_active` entry
  below; the installer is never the right answer here.
- **EvilTwin beacons but clients cannot associate** (2026-10-06). `fake_ap.py:72` does arm
  active monitor for the twin BSSID, and the client reports "Unable to connect to network".
  Ruled out by measurement: injecting beacons at EvilTwin's 10/s rate costs no measurable RX
  -- 46.4 frames/s with `MT_TXWI_ACK_CTL_REQ` set against a 43.3-55.0/s ambient spread across
  no-TX windows in the same run -- so "TX starves RX" is not the cause, and neither is the
  group-addr ACK request. Unresolved; the next step is checking whether the AP-side auth and
  assoc responses are emitted and well-formed, not more RF theorising.
- **`iw dev ... scan` returns `-EOPNOTSUPP`.** mt7601u has no cfg80211 scan in any driver, kernel
  or this port. Scanning is channel-hopping.
- **NetworkManager will revert `wlan1` to managed.** Use `nmcli device set <if> managed no` before
  monitoring. Avoid `airmon-ng check kill` — it drops the machine's internet interface.
- **The kernel `mt7601u` module must never have touched the dongle.** If it probes first, the chip
  is left in a state a userspace cold bring-up cannot recover from, and RX delivers zero bytes with
  no error at any read timeout. `modprobe -r` does *not* undo the kernel's chip init: blacklist it
  (`/etc/modprobe.d/blacklist-mt7601u.conf`) and replug. Once blacklisted, `modprobe -r` reports
  "Module not found" — use `rmmod`. Note `modprobe -r mt7601u` also stops usbmon producing output
  for *all* traffic, including a full connect; `modprobe -r usbmon && modprobe usbmon` restores it,
  so confirm a capture is non-empty before analysing it.
- **The TX ring releases its slot in `submit`, not against a status entry -- do not re-couple
  them.** `MT_TX_STAT_FIFO` is a free-running counter rather than a per-frame report, so slots
  retired against it leak: `used` ratcheted to the 64-entry cap and `submit` then refused every
  frame for good, while the endpoint still accepted 96-byte writes instantly. Measured on hardware,
  same card and frame: **65 of 400 accepted before, 400 of 400 after.** That is what stopped WEP
  FakeAuth the moment ARP replay went to burst, and it let ChopChop pass because its slower pace
  let the trickle of status entries keep up. `bulk_out_tx` is synchronous, so the frame is done
  when it returns; the kernel's `q->used` is released in an async URB completion callback that has
  no counterpart here. The ENOSPC guard stays, unreachable while the transport is synchronous, and
  `test_tx_ring.py` forces `used` to the cap to keep it covered.
- **The UI lags under this card's WEP rate, and it is not in the data path.** ~500 IVs/s produces
  ~1 s UI stalls. Ruled out by measurement, so do not re-measure: `cracker.feed` 7.7 ms per 3 s
  tick, pickling the cracker 0.1 ms at 9 KiB, `recover()` 8.6 s but in its own process,
  parse + sink 5.2 us/frame (0.3% of a core at 500 fps, headroom to ~191k fps), IV-store inserts
  O(1), replay logging on state transitions only, and the focus view on a fixed 10 Hz interval.
  Not chip-specific: this card is simply the first to drive the UI that hard.
- **Two of the six captures stop early in `verify_pcap`, and it is not a port defect.** `cal_work`,
  `mac_work` and the channel set are independent work items (`init.c:621`, `phy.c:1254`) with
  nothing serialising their register access, so they interleave register by register on the control
  pipe: `antenna/capture-1` ops 885-889 put a tune's BBP 4 access between two `mac_work` counter
  reads, `superwang/capture-2` ops 805-810 do the same with the RF CSR. A single-cursor walk cannot
  reproduce that; `verify_pcap` detects the pattern and says so rather than reporting a divergence.
- **usbmon truncates 1500-2100 of the bulk-IN records per capture** (the record length field
  exceeds the bytes kept). The RX decode phase counts and skips them; of the whole buffers, every
  chained segment decodes — ~39,000 across the six captures, none dropped by the rxwi gates.
- **capture-6 contains two descriptor variants and the port builds only one.** 7680 records carry
  `wcid=0xff byte_cnt=26`, 455 carry `wcid=0x7e ack_ctl=0x02 byte_cnt=223`. Both agree on `QSEL=2`,
  `D_PORT=0`, `TYPE=0`, `80211`, `WIV`, `rate_ctl=0x0000` and endpoint 0x07. `verify_tx` passes on
  the `wcid=0xff` form, which is what monitor injection emits; the 0x7e form needs an allocated
  WCID. The capture is truncated at 32 bytes per record, so only the info word and txwi are checked.
- **`SUPPORTED_CHANNELS` is 1-14 by decision — do not gate it on the EEPROM.** `init.c:565-571`
  derives the kernel's advertised list from `MT_EE_COUNTRY_REGION` via `ee->reg`, and
  `eeprom.py:286-314` computes `ee.reg` faithfully, but nothing reads it: wifit3 ships channel 14
  for the regions that use it, and a region-gated list would silently drop it.
- Unported, with the reason each is unreachable here: `agc_tune` (`phy.c:970`, returns early on
  `avg_rssi == 0`, a no-op without association); `tssi_cal` (`phy.c:876`), whose absence makes
  `phy.c:1009`'s skip-the-temperature-read gate a false premise, so the read runs unconditionally;
  `mt76_mac_process_rate` (`mac.c:396`), leaving `RxFrame.rate` as the raw rxwi word that the C also
  passes to `phy_get_rssi`; `MT_RXINFO_DECRYPT` / `PN_LEN` (`mac.c:473-484`), which need the key
  table; `phy.c:1180 bbp_set_bw`'s bandwidth-change path and `phy.c:289 load_bbp_temp_table_bw`,
  whose consumers are all in the unported TSSI loop; `mcu.c:519`'s persistent response IN URB, since
  this port reads each command's response synchronously, which is also what makes `mcu_cmd_deinit`
  (`mcu.c:544`) a host-side no-op with no wire counterpart.
- Deliberate deviations from the C, each the safer behaviour: `rx.py` drops a non-packet FCE type
  where `dma.c:102` only `dev_err_once`es and delivers it, there being no mac80211 downstream;
  `wcid.py:WcidAllocator` refuses cleanly where `main.c:197-203` sets the mask bit before the
  `> 119` test and permanently burns it; with `MT_RXINFO_L2PAD` set and `MPDU_LEN` exactly equal to
  the payload length the C over-reads two bytes of the FCE trailer where the port's slice clamps
  two short; `eeprom.py:rf_freq_off` is not truncated to `u8` (`eeprom.h:95`), which is masked today
  because its only consumer pushes it through an 8-bit field and becomes live only when
  `__mt7601u_phy_freq_cal` is ported.

## Driver Entry Points

- Bring-up order: `driver.py:connect` (kernel-driver refusal, interface claim, pipe map, liveness
  probe — none of it part of the kernel's register sequence) → `driver.py:_bringup` → `init.py`
  (`chip_onoff`, `wait_asic_ready`, `reset_csr_bbp`, `init_usb_dma`, `mcu_cmd_init`, **RX arming**,
  `write_mac_initvals`, `init_bbp`, `mac_start`). `_bringup` wraps `_init_hardware` so a failure
  powers the chip down and stops the reader per `init.c:413-423` before re-raising.
- Teardown: `init.py:mac_stop_hw` (`init.c:257`) — beacon-timer clear, TX/RX DMA-busy polls, the
  200x TxQ page-count drain and the RxQ drain (seven consecutive clean reads, because
  `init.c:292`'s `ok++ > 5` tests the pre-increment value). `driver.py:close()` runs it *before*
  stopping the reader, because the RxQ drain only empties while the host still consumes bulk-IN.
- Warm detection: `load_firmware` returns True when `mcu.c:422 firmware_running` found
  `MT_MCU_COM_REG0 == 1` and skipped the download; `driver.py` assigns `is_warm` from it.
- Injection descriptor: `_inject_frame` → `tx_ring.py:submit` → `tx.py:build_tx_dma`, always
  with `ack=True`; `submit` releases the ring slot as soon as the synchronous bulk-OUT returns.
  Endpoint selection: `tx.py:TX_QUEUE_INJECT` and `dma_queue_for_endpoint`.
- TX status: `tx_status.py` — `TxStatusFifo.fetch`/`drain` read-pop `MT_TX_STAT_FIFO`, drained
  inline on every submit; `driver.tx_statuses()` returns the recent window.
- ACK tally: `driver.py:_dispatch` taps raw 10-byte `FC=0xD4` buffers before the parser, which
  drops control frames. RA is `[4:10]`.
- Active Monitor: `mac.c:22-24` writes `MT_MAC_ADDR_DW0`/`DW1`; U2ME (bits 16-23 of DW1) is the
  autoresponder enable, so the chip HW-ACKs whatever MAC is programmed there. U2ME stays 0xff on
  exit, matching `mt7601u_set_macaddr`, the only place this silicon ever gets it.
- EEPROM decode: `eeprom.py` — `MT7601UEeprom.read`, `set_macaddr`. `mac.c:15 is_valid_ether_addr`
  rejects a group address as well as all-zero and installs `eth_random_addr()`; both are ported,
  and it matters because the autoresponder ACKs whatever sits in DW0/DW1.
- Firmware download: `firmware.py`, `mcu.py:MT7601UMcu.upload_firmware` (`mcu.c:356`). Reads
  `/usr/lib/firmware/mediatek/mt7601u.bin.zst`; the blob is not bundled into the PyInstaller build.
- MCU inband commands: `mcu.py` — `msg_send`, `calibrate`, `write_reg_pairs`, `burst_write_regs`.
  EP 0x8 out, 0x85 in.
- Channel tune: `phy.py:set_channel` → `rf_wr`, `set_bw_filter`, plus `apply_ch14_fixup`
  (`phy.c:322`). `driver.py:set_channel` maps the `scan` flag onto `main.c:271`/`:281`'s
  `sw_scan_start`/`sw_scan_complete` bracket, which drives `agc_reset` (`phy.c:434`).
- Calibration: `cal.py:init_cal` (`phy.c:1122`). `driver.py:_calibration_loop` is `phy.c:1014`'s
  re-queue at `MT_CALIBRATE_INTERVAL`, started per `main.c:22-25` and cancelled in `close()` per
  `main.c:37`. `cal.py:read_temp` (`phy.c:530`) costs up to 100 BBP reads every four seconds and
  the C's own comment says the busy wait rarely succeeds.
- RX decode: `rx.py:iter_frames`, `decode_segment`, `get_rssi`.
- USB pipes: `transport.py:assign_pipes`, `bulk_in_rx`, `bulk_out`, `vendor_request`.

## Scripts

All in `scripts/chips/mt7601u/`.

- `verify_pcap.py` — the registered entry point
  (`uv run python scripts/porting/verify_pcap.py mt7601u [capture]`). Drives the real
  `MT7601UDriver._bringup` over `scripts/porting/mt76_verify_replay.py`, then dispatches the
  operational tail to `phy.set_channel`, `mac.mac_work` and `cal.phy_calibrate`. Drops the EP 0x84
  RX stream (ambient air, unreproducible) so the response queue holds only EP 0x85 MCU replies.
- `verify_tx.py` — replays capture-6's recorded descriptors and asserts field-for-field equality
  against `struct mt76_txwi`.
- `verify_tune.py` — replays all 55 recorded channel tunes and asserts identical register writes.
- `verify_mt7601u.py` — superseded by `verify_pcap.py` but not yet deletable: `verify_tune.py:231`
  imports `StrictReplayTransport` and `load_ops` from it. Stages: `eeprom`, `firmware`, `wcid`.
- `gen_constants.py` — regenerates `constants.py` from `regs.h`/`mt7601u.h`; asserts one parsed
  entry per `{` in a table body.
- `gen_initvals.py` — regenerates `initvals_mac.py` / `initvals_phy.py` from the kernel tables.
- `replay_mt7601u.py` — library behind the gates: pcapng parsing plus a fake transport that serves
  recorded reads.
- `capture_mt7601u.py`, `usbmon.py` — capture helpers. `find_tunes.py` is a one-off, safe to delete.

Register-write captures must parse both vendor-request encodings: the kernel writes registers as
`bm=0xc0 br=0x07 wIndex=reg wLength=4` with the value in the data stage, this port as
`bm=0x40 br=0x02 wIndex=reg wLength=0` with the value in `wValue`. Diffing with only one form
reports the other side as silent.

`initvals_phy.py` was checked entry by entry against `initvals_phy.h` — all twelve tables match in
length, content and order, including the deliberate RF 0:12 omission and the `rf_vga` 36-40 and
`rf_channel` 50-53 gaps. `eeprom.py` was driven against a Python re-implementation of `eeprom.c`
over 4,000 synthetic EEPROMs; every decoded field matched except `rf_freq_off` above.

## Debug log

- **2026-10-06 — phantom clients are unmeasured since the shared gates were removed.** The
  subtype allowlist in `dot11/parser.py` and the ToDS-BSSID gate in `wlan/sink.py` were chip-specific
  policy in project-wide code and are gone; `MT_RX_FILTR_CFG_CRC_ERR` plus
  `rx.py:decode_segment`'s `MT_RXINFO_CRCERR` test are the chip-layer replacement. AP breadth is
  measured with those two in place and shows no phantom signature (5 and 6 APs heard exactly once,
  against the kernel's own 2 and 10). Client counts are not in the baseline harness, so whether the
  chip-layer gates alone suppress phantom *clients* has not been measured. If they return, the fix
  belongs in `chips/mt7601u/rx.py`.
